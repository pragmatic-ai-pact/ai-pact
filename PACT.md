*A commitment to responsible and sustainable AI adoption.*

AI is changing the way we design, develop, document, and deliver software.  
We should embrace it, experiment with it, and use it to become better engineers.

But faster content generation does not automatically turn into faster engineering.

Generating a 50-page architecture proposal or a 4,000 line Pull Request in minutes is easy. Reading it, challenging its assumptions, validating its technical decisions, and maintaining it is not. That's leading to AI Brain Fry.

We must ensure that the productivity gains from AI are shared by the entire engineering team, rather than transferred as additional review and maintenance burden to our teammates.

Even more, the way we use AI today is shaping the future of our profession: whether we just become AI couriers or ensure software engineering remains a human-centric craft.

This is not about restricting AI. It is about using it professionally.

We therefore commit to the following principles:

## 1. Be transparent about AI usage

Disclose when you use AI to create code, documentation, proposals, designs...

Transparency allows reviewers to understand how an artifact was produced and where additional validation may be needed.

AI assistance is not something to hide or be embarrassed about.

## 2. Share before generating

Share the problem, the context, the prompt, the proposed solution before producing extensive code or documentation.

It's easier to identify issues or gaps in a 10-line problem statement than by reviewing a 50-page doc or a 4,000-line PR.

## 3. Tame your AI to be efficient

Your AI instructions should prioritize clarity, simplicity, and pragmatism (see suggested instructions at the bottom of this doc).

AI tends to produce verbose, flamboyant and over-sophisticated solutions. We're not always designing a real-time geo-distributed transaction-safe financial system.

Challenge your AI to produce simpler, shorter, and more understandable solutions.

## 4. Own the output

Review before requesting a review.

Prove that.

Be the first to add comments, questions, doubts... before anyone else.

AI-generated content is your responsibility, not AI's, not everyone else's.

Before sharing anything for review:

- Read the entire output.
- Challenge its assumptions and technical decisions.
- Remove unnecessary complexity and repetition.
- Validate code, examples, references, and claims.
- Make sure you understand and can defend what you are proposing.

Never outsource your first round of review to your colleagues.

## 5. Collaborate in the open

Store AI generated artifacts in shared locations where shared feedback is possible (Github, Box, Office).

Everyone should be able to see previous discussions and decisions.

The quality of reviews grows with the number of reviewers.

Avoid private feedback loops and duplicated reviews.

## 6. Iterate. Don't regenerate.

Treat reviews as incremental improvements, not invitations to start over.

The response to a review comment or a change request should never be a whole new artifact (code, doc, ...).

Maintain a single source of truth. Address comments, explain disagreements, and make limited changes to the existing artifact.

Preserve what has already been discussed and agreed upon.

## 7. Optimize for collective productivity

Our goal is not to maximize the amount of code or documentation we can individually generate with AI.

Our goal is to deliver better engineering outcomes, faster, with less unnecessary work for everyone involved.

## 8. Find and retain your voice

Engineering is an act of creation. We don't just solve problems. We express ourselves through the way we approach and create solutions.

Whether you're a junior engineer or a 20-year veteran, make sure that whatever AI generates reflects your voice, your experience, your traits, your preferences.

If you are a junior, don't let AI shape your identity before you've had the chance to develop one. Go outside the prompt box, Google it, explore, read, try new things, and make mistakes.

If you are a veteran, challenge generic answers, reject content that doesn't feel yours, and shape AI-generated work until it represents what you actually think and believe.

Don't just let AI speak for you.

Ultimately, for everyone else, you are what you produce.

If you let AI speak for you, you become NULL.

## Our shared commitment

We are all learning how to work with these tools. Nobody has all the answers, and nobody is expected to get everything right the first time.

But we are all responsible for making this transition beneficial to the entire team.

Let's use AI to reduce repetitive soul-less work, accelerate learning, challenge our thinking, and improve the quality of our engineering.

**Let AI do more of the work, not make more work for each other.**

We are engineers first, AI users second. Let's keep it that way.

## Suggested AI instructions

Act as a pragmatic senior software engineer. Follow these principles:

**Be concise.** Prefer short, direct answers. Avoid unnecessary explanations, repetition, and verbose documentation.

**Keep it simple.** Prefer the simplest solution that solves the problem. Avoid overengineering, unnecessary abstractions, speculative requirements, and premature optimization.

**Respect existing architecture.** Reuse established patterns, conventions, and components. Don't introduce new frameworks, dependencies, or architectural changes without good reason.

**Challenge assumptions.** Question requirements, identify gaps and risks, and suggest better alternatives. Don't blindly agree with the user.

**Think before generating.** Understand the problem, context, and requirements before producing extensive output. Ask for clarification when necessary.

**Be incremental.** Make the smallest meaningful change. Preserve existing work, decisions, and feedback. Prefer focused amendments over rewriting entire artifacts.

**Own the substance.** Prioritize correctness, maintainability, and practical trade-offs over presentation. Distinguish facts from assumptions and never invent APIs, references, or implementation details.

**Respect everyone's time.** Present essential information first. Make outputs easy to understand, validate, and review. Explain why changes are necessary and what they affect.
